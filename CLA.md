## How to sign

A bot comments on your first pull request and asks you to reply with the sentence it
gives you. One signature covers every Contribution you make afterwards. Signatures are
recorded in [`signatures/cla.json`](./signatures/cla.json) in this repository, with the
GitHub account, the date and the pull request that prompted it.

## Note for thaink², to be removed once addressed

This text is an adaptation of the ASF Individual CLA v2.2. Three points deserve a lawyer's
eye before this is relied on:

1. **Governing law and jurisdiction are absent.** The ASF text omits them because the ASF
   is a US foundation; a French company should state them.
2. **Section 4 covers individuals only.** A contributor whose employer holds the rights
   needs a *Corporate* CLA, which does not exist yet.
3. **Signing by bot comment** is the common practice, but its evidential value has not
   been assessed here. A signature is a contract; keeping the audit trail
   (`signatures/cla.json`, commit history) matters.

A lighter alternative exists and needs no contract: the **DCO** (Developer Certificate of
Origin), a `Signed-off-by:` line in each commit. It covers section 4 — the point that
matters most often — and neither section 2 nor 3.
